01

The problem

One sentence in the user's position, no mechanism in it. Then one sentence on what it costs today, in money, time or trust.

As a logistics planner, I find out something has gone wrong with my shipment hours or days after it happened, buried in an email thread, and I am not sure what I am supposed to do about it.

Every hour of delay before I act is an hour of lost slack against my must-arrive-by date: demurrage and storage accrue on a customs hold, a missed vessel pushes delivery by a sailing (often a week, assumed), and my ops contact spends their time relaying status instead of resolving it.

03

Goal and non-goals

Goal. An exceptions dashboard that shows the planner every open exception on their shipments as it happens, and sends them to the specific part of the customer portal where they act on it, instead of waiting for an email.

  • Primary exceptions: customs hold, missed vessel (rolled or cut off).
  • Secondary exception: documentation error (packing list, customs declaration), detected by supporting systems.
  • Information fidelity check: each exception shows its source and when it was last confirmed; if sources disagree, the dashboard says so rather than picking one.
  • Layout: the three exceptions as columns, one card per shipment showing affected containers and age of the exception. Alternative worth testing: a per-shipment timeline swimlane, which handles a shipment carrying two exceptions better than columns do.
  • Time left to act: every card carries one call to action, linked to the portal page that resolves it and coloured by time left before the action window closes: under 1h, 1h to 6h, 6h to 24h, over 24h (tiers assumed). Cards sort soonest deadline first, so a customs hold due in 45 minutes outranks a document error due in 3 days.
  • Everything else: a free-text summary field, capped at 150 words on screen with the full text kept in backend logs. Example: cargo ready date now later than the must-arrive-by date.
  • Suggested actions: for each primary or secondary exception, up to 3 options (count assumed) retrieved from the resolution playbook (RAG), each citing its source and linking to the portal page. If retrieval is not confident, no suggestion: the card routes to ops instead.

Non-goals for v1

  • Not building notifications (email, SMS, push), because the dashboard has to be trusted before we push it at people.
  • Not quantifying the commercial impact ($ value of an exception), because it needs landed cost and contract data we do not need to prove the core loop.
  • Not resolving exceptions inside the dashboard, because the portal already has the flows; we redirect to them rather than rebuild them.
Assumptions I am making, correct me

1. Exception events, document images and electronic data are available and ready; we are not building ingestion.   2. Every redirection lands on a portal page where the planner can actually act.   3. Ops stays the backstop for anything the dashboard abstains on, and has time to annotate corrections, which become the eval set for suggested actions.

Prototype

What the review changed: exceptions are not equally urgent, so every card now shows time left to act as a colour-coded chip (under 1h, 1h to 6h, 6h to 24h, over 24h) with a separate action button to the portal page, sorted soonest first (R7, N4).

  • 1. Exceptions board (normal). Four columns, one card per shipment with affected containers, age, source and last confirmed; one shipment sits in two columns to show the grain trap; each card has a call to action coloured by time left (red under 1h to green over 24h, grey when ops owns it); the detail panel shows 3 cited suggested actions with portal links (R1 to R3, R7).
  • 2. System abstains (uncertain). Broker says Hold, carrier says Released: the card is flagged unconfirmed on customs status, suggestions are withheld with the reason, and ops will advise (R4, R5).
  • 3. Correction and audit (ops). Ops marks a suggestion wrong, records the action that actually resolved it, the audit trail adds it to the eval set, and the kill switch shows wrong rate per type against the 5% threshold (R6, N3).

Requirements

Condition, then observable behaviour, then how we would test it. If I cannot name the test, it is not a requirement yet, it is an intention.

IDRequirementTest
R1When a planner opens the dashboard, the system must show every open exception in exactly one of four columns (customs hold, missed vessel, document error, other), one card per shipment, with affected container count and exception age.Seeded fixture of shipments with known exceptions, including one shipment with two types. Every exception appears once; counts match the fixture.
R2When an "other" summary exceeds 150 words, the system must show the first 150 words with a truncation marker and write the full text to the backend log keyed by exception ID.Boundary inputs of 149, 150, 151 and 2,000 words. Check on-screen word count and that the log holds the full text.
R3When a primary or secondary exception is open, the system must show up to 3 suggested actions, each citing the playbook passage it came from and linking to the portal page for that shipment.Ops-annotated set of past exceptions with the action that resolved them. Report recall@3, MRR and precision@3; every suggestion has a citation.
R4When the top retrieval score is below the cutoff, or the exception type is "other", the system must show no suggestion and route the card to ops, saying which it is unsure of: the exception type or the action.Out-of-corpus cases mixed into the eval set. Abstention fires on them; false abstentions on in-corpus cases reported separately.
R5When sources disagree on an exception's status, or the last confirmation is older than the freshness window, the system must flag the card unconfirmed and name the field in doubt.Fixtures with conflicting broker and carrier events, and stale events. Flag appears and names the field.
R6When ops marks a suggestion wrong, the system must record the correct action, the annotator and the time, and add the case to the eval set for the next release.Annotate a sample in staging; check the audit record and that the eval set grows by that count.
R7When an exception has a known action deadline, the card must show time left as text and a call to action coloured by tier (under 1h, 1h to 6h, 6h to 24h, over 24h), sort soonest first within its column, and move tier without a page reload as the clock runs.Fixture deadlines at 59m, 1h, 1h01m, 5h59m, 6h, 23h59m, 24h, 3d and unknown; advance a mocked clock and check tier, text and order at each boundary.

What the system must not do

IDConstraintTrigger
N1Must not take any action in the portal. Suggestions are links only; the planner acts.Revisited only when the ops-marked-correct rate holds above target for a full quarter, not on a date
N2Must not show a suggested action without a cited playbook passage.Any uncited suggestion is a release blocker
N3Must not degrade silently. If ops marks more than 5% of suggestions wrong for an exception type over a rolling 7 days (both assumed, set from baseline), suggestions for that type switch off, cards route to ops, and ops is alerted within 1 hour (assumed).Rolling 7-day window, per exception type
N4Must not show a calm tier when the deadline is unknown or its source is stale. Show "deadline unknown" in grey and route to ops.Any card without a confirmed deadline source
Four numbers I would set deliberately rather than inherit

The confidence cutoff for acting alone, the accuracy target for the decision itself, the error-rate limit and its window, and how quickly a human has to be told. I would not set any of them in this room. I would set them from the cost of being wrong in each direction, and I would want the first version deliberately too conservative.

Metrics

TypeMetricWhy this one
LeadingInformation correctness: share of displayed exceptions whose type, shipment and status match the source system on a weekly ops audit sample. Retrieval components: MRR, recall@K and precision@K (K = 3) on the ops-annotated eval set.Moves in days, tells you the mechanism works
LaggingCorrect actions suggested per 1,000 suggestions shown. Correct means ops annotation says the suggestion matches the action that actually resolved the exception.The business outcome, moves in a quarter
CounterWrong-action follow-through per 1,000 actions taken: planner followed a suggestion and ops later had to reverse or redo it. Watched next to ops tickets per 1,000 shipments, which should fall, not just move.Goes up if we succeeded in a harmful way. Throughput always improves if you accept more wrong answers

If the metric drops after launch: segment first, name the substitution hypothesis explicitly, check it against the aggregate rather than the component, then say what evidence would separate the competing explanations.
